ENGLISH · GRADE 8–10

Reading between the lines.

You don't need a dictionary for every new word. The words around it often give you clues.

What are context clues?

Context clues are hints in a sentence or paragraph that help you understand an unfamiliar word. Readers use them to figure out meaning without stopping every time.

Look for explanations

Sometimes a writer explains the word nearby: “The arid, or very dry, land had few plants.” The phrase “very dry” tells us that arid means dry.

Look for examples

Examples can show what a word means: “Nocturnal animals, such as owls and bats, are active at night.” The examples help us work out that nocturnal means active at night.

Look for opposites

Words like but, although, or unlike may point to a contrast: “Unlike her talkative brother, Amahle was reserved and said very little.” The contrast suggests that reserved means quiet or not very talkative.

Your turn

“After cycling all morning, Sipho was famished. He ate two sandwiches and an apple.” What do you think famished means?

Answer: very hungry. The food he ate is your clue!
