The water cycle.

Water is always on the move. The sun helps it travel from the ground, into the sky, and back again.

1. Evaporation: up it goes

The sun warms water in oceans, rivers, dams, and puddles. Some of it changes from liquid water into an invisible gas called water vapour. This is evaporation.

Liquid water+ sunWater vapour ↑Heat gives water the energy to rise into the air.

2. Condensation: clouds form

Higher in the sky, the air is cooler. Water vapour cools and changes into tiny liquid droplets. The droplets gather to form clouds — that change is called condensation.

3. Precipitation: back to Earth

Cloud droplets bump into one another and grow. When they become heavy enough, they fall as precipitation: rain, hail, sleet, or snow.

4. Collection: the journey begins again

Water flows into rivers, dams, and oceans, or soaks into the ground. Then the sun warms it and the cycle starts over. Plants also release water vapour from their leaves — a process called transpiration.

Quick check

What is the name for water vapour cooling down and forming tiny droplets?

Answer: condensation. Clouds are made of these tiny droplets!
